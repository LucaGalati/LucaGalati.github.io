Information has to pay for itself

Picture a trader deciding whether to pay for research into where the FTSE 100 is heading. What she learns is worth something only to whoever trades on it before everyone else knows. If her block is published an hour after she trades, she keeps most of that value; if it is published at once, others see her trade and most of the value is gone.

Move the cost of the research and switch the rule. When research pays, she trades, and the price learns the news, an hour late. When it does not pay, nobody does the research, and no block ever brings that news to the price.

This is the textbook argument of Grossman and Stiglitz (1980), which the paper’s conclusion invokes: prices must stay noisy enough for information to pay for itself.
