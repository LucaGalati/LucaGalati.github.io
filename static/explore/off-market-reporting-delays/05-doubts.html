From 12,912 blocks to 3,466

The data come from Refinitiv’s Tick History: every on-screen trade and quote in ICE’s FTSE 100 futures, stamped to the thousandth of a second, plus a separate record of blocks, from 21 August 2017 to 17 August 2018, close to six months either side of the rule change.

Two filters do most of the work. Blocks in the ten trading days before each contract’s delivery are dropped, because many of them are traders rolling positions from one contract month to another rather than trading on news. Blocks that span several contract months, “strategy” blocks, are dropped too. The authors report that keeping either gives similar results.

A small comparison group of bigger trades

A comparison group earns its keep by resembling the treated group in everything but the treatment. Here the resemblance is partial. The blocks above 3,328 lots are roughly four times the size of a typical experimental block, and there are about a tenth as many. The authors say so themselves, calling it “an important albeit limited form of control”.

What the larger blocks do show is a lasting move around publication in both half-years: buy-like and sell-like alike, over either window, it is significant at the 1% level. Blocks that kept their hour went on carrying news.

What the prices cannot tell

  • Who leftThe exit of informed traders is read from prices. The paper does not observe who traded the blocks, or where they went after the change.
  • One marketOne contract on one exchange over one year. The rule change came in the wake of MiFID II, in force from 3 January 2018; the control group is the guard against changes of that kind, and the six-month halves are meant to keep seasonal patterns alike.
  • DirectionWhether a block was a buy or a sell is inferred from its price, not recorded.
  • ChecksKeeping strategy blocks or the days before delivery is reported to give similar results, available from the authors on request.
