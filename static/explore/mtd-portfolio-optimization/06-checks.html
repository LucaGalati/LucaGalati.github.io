A second way to score stocks agrees, mostly

There is more than one way to give each stock an assortativity score. The paper reruns everything with a second one (Sabek and Pigorsch, 2023), which scores each arrow by its own contribution to the correlation and adds up a stock’s arrows.

Switch the grid between the two measures and the two yardsticks. With the second measure the wins against the classic recipe go from seven of twelve to five, a performance the paper calls good but “comparatively less consistent”. In–Out stays the most reliable rule, five wins in six across the two measures; Out–Out never wins.

In sample, the classic recipe sits at or near the top

Appendix 2 measures the same portfolios on the very windows they were fitted to. Every Sharpe ratio there lies between 2.44 and 3.54, two and a half to four and a half times the figures for the 30 days that followed: a portfolio tends to look good on the past it was chosen from.

In every market the classic recipe is first or second there, as one would expect: it is built to strike the best balance of return and risk on exactly that window, so any tilt has to give something up there. Over the next 30 days, which the model never saw, In–Out goes from last to ahead of the classic recipe in all three markets.
