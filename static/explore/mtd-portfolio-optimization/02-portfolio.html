Seek some links, avoid others

To choose stocks the paper needs a score for each stock, not one number for the whole network. A stock’s local assortativity is its share of the network’s assortativity (the tilt from the last slide), computed from the arrows it sends; the ten shares add up to the whole. Then comes a choice the paper makes on structural and economic grounds: two modalities are sought, two avoided.

In–In maximize the score

Stocks that mostly absorb influence, each from its own sources rather than the same ones.

In–Out minimize the score

A much-led stock tied to a strong leader, where a shock can echo back and forth. Prefer clearly different roles.

Out–In maximize the score

Leaders feeding followers: a one-way, top-down flow with few loops back.

Out–Out minimize the score

Leaders tied to leaders: a club of dominant stocks that move together.

In each name the first word judges the arrow’s source, the second its target. The sought pair judges the target by how much it is led; the avoided pair, by how much it leads.

Section 2.5, pages 5–6. Local assortativity, equation (9), extends Piraveenan, Prokopenko and Zomaya (2010) to directed, weighted networks. The paper also reads its Figure 2, where In–In and Out–In scores fall as stocks lead more while In–Out and Out–Out rise, as support for this pairing (page 9).

Place your bet: how many stocks?

The classic recipe is Markowitz’s: weights that balance each stock’s expected return against the portfolio’s risk, with no short selling and the weights adding up to one. The network rules start from the same recipe.

The network term moves money between a few stocks

In full, the classic recipe picks the weights that maximize expected return minus half of δ times the portfolio’s variance. The paper sets δ = 10, a moderate-to-high dislike of risk.

The network version adds one more term: each stock’s score times its weight, added under In–In and Out–In, subtracted under In–Out and Out–Out. Here are four invented stocks with In–In scores. Turn the term up and see where the money goes.

Deck 2 · From network to portfolio · 5 of 5

Twenty years, about 160 fresh starts

Each test fits everything on a window of past days, the network, the scores, average returns and risks, then holds the portfolio for the next 30 trading days, which the model never saw. Then the window rolls forward 30 days and everything is fitted again. Daily closing prices run from December 2004 to December 2024: more than 5,000 days, about 160 windows.

The window is three times the number in the index’s name, because the model is hungry: with three states and n stocks it has n²·3·2 + n(n − 1) numbers to estimate.

Every network portfolio faces two yardsticks: the classic recipe, and the same recipe tilted by scores from an ordinary correlation network, without arrows, built on the same window.
