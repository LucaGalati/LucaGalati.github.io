What the test leaves out

  • CostsPortfolios are rebuilt every 30 trading days; trading costs lie outside what the paper measures.
  • CashThe risk-free rate is set to zero “for convenience” (page 8), so a Sharpe ratio here is average return per unit of volatility.
  • Which stocks28, 46 and 78 stocks from three large indices. The authors present the method as suited to small and medium universes, up to around 100 assets, a size common in practice (page 2).
  • SizeEach window the model estimates 5,460 numbers for the Dow and 42,510 for the FTSE 100. The authors find their results “slightly less consistent” in bigger markets, tie this to the model’s computational cost, and note that practitioners usually work with small batches of stocks (page 10).

Held fixed throughout: one dislike of risk, δ = 10, which the paper calls moderate to high, within the usual range of 5 to 15, and one cut-off for a flat day, half a standard deviation. The authors report that a test on a Singapore index left the results “qualitatively unchanged” (pages 7 and 9).
