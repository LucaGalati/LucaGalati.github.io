II · 1 The measurement problem

Nothing to discover, everything to synchronise.

Textbook measures of price discovery ask who learns first. They assume that some trader knows something the others do not, and that prices reveal it trade by trade. A top-rated bond has no such secrets to speak of: what moves it is announced to everyone at once.

So the paper asks a different question — how efficiently is public information absorbed across venues? — and looks for two symptoms of inefficiency: screens that disagree with each other, and price moves that predictably undo themselves.

The first symptom

II · 2 The first symptom

Screens that disagree.

Here three screens price the same bond through one trading day. The pale band is its true value, moved by public news; the lines are what the screens show. Make them catch up less often, and they stop agreeing.

The paper's first gauge, synchronisation, measures how closely the venues' prices move together: a statistical cousin of the toy's share of time all screens agree. In this lab, 1 means lockstep.

The second symptom

II · 3 The second symptom

Moves that undo themselves.

Now let the screens misjudge the news. Overshoot, and prices go too far and drift back; move only part of the way, and they creep after it. Either way the next move becomes predictable, which public news should not allow.

Two gauges catch it. The variance ratio compares a long move with the short steps inside it — here, an hour and its twelve five-minute steps: a random walk scores 1; below 1, short moves partly reverse; above 1, they keep going. Autocorrelation asks whether one short move predicts the next: 0 means it does not.

The paper folds these two gauges and synchronisation into one score of public-information efficiency.

II · 5 The decomposition

What moved the score.

Back to the first question: do the screens agree? Not fully — and after the arrangement, EU-Bonds' screens agreed more closely than before, relative to the controls'. Stronger synchronisation across venues drove most of the 4.3 points.

The other two gauges point the same way, in the paper's reading: its variance-ratio and autocorrelation estimates also indicate more efficient pricing.

Where the flow went

II · 6 The mechanism

Concentrated quotes, not fewer screens.

The arrangement closed no venue: the other exchanges kept trading. What changed is where dealers had to keep quotes alive — and the order flow followed. The paper finds that it concentrated on the designated venues.

That is what centralising means here. Not gathering the market into one place, but giving it designated screens that are always quoted and that draw the flow.
