Six fixes a day, rebuilt from the order book

Nobody ever published a ten- or twenty-minute fix, so the authors had to build them. To compare like with like, they rebuilt the five-minute fix too, the same way, rather than taking the published one. Each window gets a second version that keeps every print, skipping the checks that throw out outliers.

Each day then gives three gaps per window, and each pair and year a root mean square of them, the typical size of the gap. The tests compare windows on those daily gaps.

Refinitiv runs the fix, and its data cover every new order, cancellation and amendment on the platforms the fix draws on, with the best bid and ask on each. The data cannot be shared; the code can, except the part that rebuilds the fix, which the agreement with Refinitiv keeps private.

The tests were promised in advance

Most studies are judged once the results are in. This one was not: its questions, data and tests were reviewed and published in the same journal in 2024, before any results existed. This article reports what came out and where it departed from the plan.

Tests fixed before anyone has seen the data cannot have been chosen because they worked; that is why the paper keeps its registered, “confirmatory” analysis apart from the rest.
