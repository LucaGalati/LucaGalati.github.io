Two summaries of the same daily gaps are at work here: how big they typically are, which the chart shows, and where they sit on average, which the paper’s tests compare. Those tests find no shift large enough to rule out chance for four of the six pairs; for EUR/USD and GBP/USD the average gap moves by about a tenth of a pip at twenty minutes on normal days.

A trade-off between matching and robustness

The third yardstick, robustness, is the gap between the fix and the same fix computed without its outlier checks. It grows with the window too.

The authors read a wider gap to the unchecked fix as greater robustness. Read that way, a longer window could buy a little robustness and costs attainability, the trade-off an earlier study for the UK regulator also found.

Changing the window would also mean re-assessing the surveillance algorithms that look for manipulation. Weighing both, the authors conclude that five minutes “remains broadly effective”.

Year by year, the differences fade into chance

Pool the six pairs and run the same tests one year at a time. Each mark is a t-statistic: how far the average daily gap shifts when the window grows, measured against its own noise. The further from zero, the harder it is to put the shift down to chance; filled marks are the ones the paper marks significant, more solidly for the stricter levels.

On all days, the shifts significant at 5% or 1% all fall between 2015 and 2018; later years carry at most a 10% mark. At month-end the marked shifts fall mostly in 2017 and 2018. In the authors’ reading the five-minute window holds up in most years, the exceptions being 2016 on normal days and 2017 at month-end.
