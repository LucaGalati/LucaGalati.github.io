How a price can fall 1,500 per cent

A price cannot fall more than 100%. But the paper adds up minute-by-minute log returns, the natural log of each price ratio, and that sum has no floor: −300% means the price ended at e−3, about a twentieth of where it began, and each further −300% divides by about twenty again.

Abnormal means against what was expected: no change for a stablecoin, and for bitcoin its average return over the sample’s first two days.

LUNA’s CAR of about −1,500% is five such steps of −300%. A CAR is not a price, though: it adds up abnormal returns on minute prices averaged across exchanges.

Where the findings stop

The authors set out the limits of what one episode, measured this way, can show.

  • Averaged pricesPrices are a weighted average across exchanges. The authors note that this smooths the size of the moves, and prefer it because small venues then carry less weight. p.7
  • The start of the collapseThe collapse period starts at midnight on 10 May, the day of the first news of a possible crash. The authors note that the start of a crisis is not always easy to date. pp.7–8
  • One eventForty days around one collapse. The authors say their findings cannot be extended to calmer, more normal markets. p.15
  • Herding, a likely partial causeThe authors offer herding as a likely part of the cause, suggested by how differently the coins behaved and by the cascade in the event study. abstract, pp.2, 10
