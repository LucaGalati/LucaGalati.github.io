A dollar is not always a dollar

A stablecoin is a crypto token built to be worth exactly one US dollar, always. Traders call that promise the peg.

TerraUSD, or UST, was one of the biggest, with about $18 billion of coins outstanding. Around midnight on 9 May 2022 it began to slip. By nine the next morning it sold for 79 cents.

Over the following days the other big dollar coins moved too. Some sold for less than a dollar. Others, stranger still, sold for more. They all make the same promise. So why would anyone pay more than a dollar for a dollar, and why would anyone sell one for less?

Same promise, five designs

A coin can only promise a dollar if something stands behind it, so that a holder who hands one back gets a dollar out. What that something is differs sharply from coin to coin.

Cash in a bank. Short-dated US Treasuries. A mixed pool that includes commercial paper, short-term company debt. Crypto deposited as collateral, worth more than the coins issued against it. Or, for UST, mostly an algorithm and a sister token.

A run hurts each design differently

A run is many holders asking for their dollar at once. Set the size of the run and how far crypto prices fall that day, and watch three designs take it: cash in a vault, crypto collateral of $1.45 per coin, and an algorithm that pays out in a sister token.

Past a design’s line, the holders no longer covered run too, and the coin slides away from the dollar.

Try a run of 60% with no fall in crypto. Then a 40% fall with no run.

Why does the algorithm break on a run alone? →

Toy model, illustrative numbers, not from the paper. 100 coins; a redemption pays $1 while backing lasts, then the uncovered share runs, for up to 12 rounds. Cash: $100. Collateral: $145 (MakerDAO’s ratio, p.18), falling with crypto. Algorithmic: a sister token worth $150, more than all the coins, so it starts with the largest cushion; it falls with crypto and loses another $1 for every $1 paid out. Fees and real defences of a peg are left out.

Then it happened for real

In May 2022 the toy’s ingredients arrived together. UST’s holders ran. LUNA, the sister token meant to absorb them, collapsed: $1,000 of LUNA at its price a month earlier was worth about five cents on 15 May.

UST did have a reserve: about 80,000 bitcoin, worth some $2.4 billion on 7 May, held by the Luna Foundation Guard. Nearly all of it was spent, apparently in an effort to hold the peg. The paper puts the ecosystem that came down at $60 billion.

UST was one coin of five, and the other four were built differently. What did they do while it sank?
