A straight road, and a detour through innovation

The paper’s story runs in four steps:

  1. Women in the executive team bring other ways of weighing risky, long-horizon projects; earlier work finds female executives less overconfident (p. 2).
  2. Inclusive practices lower the friction between people who differ, so their ideas get combined rather than kept apart (p. 2–3).
  3. Combined ideas become innovation: patents, and products and processes that cut customers’ environmental costs (p. 2).
  4. Investors read innovation as a promise of future earnings; the paper expects profits to follow more slowly (p. 3, 6).

the straight roadthe detour through innovation

If the story holds, part of what D&I is worth reaches performance only by way of innovation: the detour. Everything else takes the straight road.

Two detours, two destinations

The detour has two legs: how strongly D&I goes with innovation, and how strongly innovation goes with performance. What it carries is their product. Below the map each detour becomes a rectangle: the first leg is its width, the second its height, and its area is what it carries.

Switch to environmental innovation. Its first leg is wider, since D&I goes with more of it, counted in standard deviations. Its second is less than half as high, so the rectangle holds less: 7.5% of the link with Tobin’s Q, against 11.2% for patents. A stronger first leg helps only if the second is not much weaker.
