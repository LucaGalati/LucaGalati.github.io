Diversity is counted where decisions are made

Most studies count women on the board. But a board meets periodically to monitor and advise; the executives (the chief executive, the finance chief and the other officers) decide budgets, research and which projects go ahead (p. 3). A firm can have a diverse board and an all-male executive suite, so the paper counts women among the executives.

That share is low in most firms: in at least a quarter of the 47,212 firm-years no executive is a woman, the median is 11%, and three in four stay at or below 22%.

What the measure cannot see: it records gender, not differences in values, experience or ways of thinking (footnote 2), and gender is one dimension among many; ethnic, cultural, educational and age diversity are left to future work (Section 6.2).

Inclusion is five practices, counted

Following Dey and co-authors (2023), the paper counts which of five practices a firm reports and divides by five. Three firm-years in four report at most two.

A list of policies is not the same as feeling included: the paper sees what firms disclose, not what employees experience (footnote 7). Employee surveys would be richer but would shrink an international sample. Refinitiv’s own D&I scores were set aside as less transparent and available only from 2016; used as a check, they leave the conclusions unchanged except for a weaker environmental-innovation detour to return on assets (p. 13).

A property of the average: inclusion varies more than diversity, so the D&I score moves mostly with it.
