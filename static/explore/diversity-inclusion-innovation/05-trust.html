47,212 firm-years, after a long funnel

The paper starts from every firm-year in Refinitiv’s ESG universe from 2010 to 2023 and keeps those with the data its models need.

The biggest cut is missing D&I data: the sample is made of firms whose diversity and inclusion are on record. What remains is 6,948 firms in 55 countries and 45 sectors, 2010–2021. About a third of the observations are American and a tenth Japanese; one robustness check drops both.

Like for like can flip a sign

Every comparison in the paper, across its 47,212 firm-years, is between firms that look alike on size, leverage, sales growth, board size, independence and tenure, stock volatility, intangible assets, cash flow, capital spending and R&D, after average differences between years, sectors and countries are removed. That matters, because raw comparisons can point the other way:

Side path · Can it be trusted? · 3 of 4

Which way does the arrow point?

The timing helps: D&I is measured two years before performance. But firms that were already doing well may be the ones that invest in D&I, and good performance tends to persist (p. 14, 18).

One check aims at this worry directly: it adds last period’s performance as a control, so that firms already doing well are compared with each other; the conclusions hold (p. 14). Another aims at the second leg, from innovation to performance. It predicts innovation from how generously a country’s tax system subsidises business R&D, a choice made by legislators rather than by any one firm, so that innovation is not simply a by-product of success; the results stay qualitatively the same (p. 13). The authors present it as a sensitivity check, not their main identification (footnote 5). Both are reported in words only.
