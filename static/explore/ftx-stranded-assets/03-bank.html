An exchange that is also a bank

Buy a share in London or New York and the exchange never touches your money. A broker keeps your account; the exchange only matches orders.

FTX did it all in one place: it matched orders, kept your account, turned dollars into coins and held those coins, as a bank holds deposits. So a halt on withdrawals could strand customers’ money while trading carried on.

Add its own token, FTT, and a sister trading firm, Alameda Research, and the paper sees an exchange, broker, dealer, token issuer and hedge fund rolled into one, with little of the oversight a stock exchange faces.

FTT seems to have been priced as if all of it could be sold

FTT gave holders perks on FTX and promised that its supply would shrink through regular “burns”, destroyed tokens, much like a share buyback. Its market value rose from $0.54 billion at the start of 2021 to $9.61 billion that September.

Around 6 November 2022 the tokens actually on the market were worth about $3 billion, while the “fully diluted” value, counting every FTT in existence, was about $8 billion. The paper suggests the gap was tokens FTX kept off the market while valuing them as if they could be sold at market prices.

Customers could not tell how much FTX could pay back, and FTX could not tell how many would ask: the paper’s picture of a bank run driven by what nobody could see.
