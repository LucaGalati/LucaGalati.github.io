Binance as the yardstick

Each result sets the average over the four days before the freeze against the average over the four days after, for one coin, one measure and one exchange, with a t-test for whether the difference could be chance.

If FTX moves and Binance does not, the move is put down to the freeze. Binance is the natural yardstick: the same coins on the same days, by far the largest exchange, and its customers could still withdraw.

The catch, which the paper names itself: a collapse this size can spread. On Binance, Bitcoin’s and Ether’s spreads widened significantly too, and depth changed for every coin. So the comparison sets the frozen exchange against a market that was itself shaken, and measures how much more things changed on FTX.

What the comparison cannot tell

  • Two averagesEach result compares two four-day means with a t-test, one measure at a time. Nothing else that moved that week is held fixed.
  • A moving yardstickBinance felt the shock too, a spillover in the paper’s reading, so FTX is set against a market that was itself shaken.
  • Price unitsThe quoted spread is ask minus bid, a price gap. FTT lost 89% of its price between 4 and 11 November, so the same gap was a larger share of it; the paper reports that versions relative to price gave qualitatively similar results.
  • One eventA single collapse, about four days each side; most coins traded most on the first day of the freeze, and a four-day average sums up the whole window.
  • Who tradedThere are no trader identities. Large average transactions on one FTX wallet hint at institutions; the paper calls this suggestive.
  • WhyThe flight to safety is the authors’ reading of the selling pressure and the turnover; they say traders’ motives need further analysis.
