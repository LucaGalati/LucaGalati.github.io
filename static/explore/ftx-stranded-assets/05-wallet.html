Follow one FTX wallet

On a public blockchain every transfer is visible, even when its owners are not. The authors followed one Ethereum wallet owned by FTX: 14,826 transactions between 8 and 12 November.

Step through the days. On 9 November every one of the 776 transactions brought money in, just what a halt on withdrawals would look like. On the 10th about $12 million went out, possibly to the Bahamian customers FTX said it was paying. On the last day more than half a billion dollars left.

Average sizes are the paper’s clue to who was moving money: small traders cannot assemble a large transaction, so large averages point to institutions. A hint, not proof.

The last night

In the early hours of 12 November, between 02:23 and 02:43, $218.2 million left one FTX-owned Ethereum wallet that the authors tracked, nearly all of it to a single address that Etherscan marks with a warning. At 03:28 UTC trading on FTX stopped.

The rest of that day’s outflow, about $300 million, went after the bankruptcy filing and the halt; some attribute it to a hack. Where the money went lies beyond the paper’s scope; the authors leave the trail, and the Bahamian withdrawals, for further study.
