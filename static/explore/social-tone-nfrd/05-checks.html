Across seven models, the change in slope stays near −0.06

The paper re-estimates its model on the same 329 reports, adding a Covid-19 switch, a separate effect for each year, or three measures of family control. The change in slope after 2017 stays between −0.058 and −0.065, each time significant at the 5% level.

The slope before 2017 stays between 0.093 and 0.101, each time significant at the 1% level. The authors read the added controls as leaving their main findings unchanged.

The comparison runs across firms, not within them

The paper compares firms with other firms in the same industry. In a check it reports without a table, it compares each firm only with itself, year by year, using firm effects. Then the results do not hold.

The authors suggest why: a small sample, scores that move little within a firm, or firm traits nobody observes. Either way, the evidence rests on differences between firms.

What held, and what is left open

Held

  • A Covid-19 switch, and a separate effect for every year Table 5
  • Three measures of family control, added one at a time and all together Table 6
  • Dropping financial, energy and utility firms footnote 6, untabulated
  • Social tone measured in logs footnote 9, untabulated
  • No significant change in tone in the run-up to the rule footnote 5, untabulated

Left open

  • Comparing each firm only with itself: the results do not hold footnote 8
  • Direction: firms that do well may simply write more footnote 10
  • Quantity, not quality: every listed word counts the same, substance or not p.75
  • A small sample from one country: 83 firms, and about 60 reports from before the rule p.78, Tables 1, 3
