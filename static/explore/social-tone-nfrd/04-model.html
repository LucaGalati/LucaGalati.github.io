One equation, one switch, one change of slope

Do big firms simply write more about people? No: size and social tone barely move together (correlation 0.05, not significant). Big firms do score higher, though (0.49), so the comparison holds size fixed.

Social score = β1 × tone + β2 × after + β3 × tone × after + controls + industry effect + noise

After switches on from 2017. β1 is the slope before the rule; β3, the paper’s variable of interest, is the change in slope after it; β2 shifts the line at zero tone.

  • Held fixedsize, profitability, debt, cash, tangible assets, market-to-book, an audited report, net tone, the firm’s industry; Covid-19 and year effects in later columns.

What goes wrong without them? Raw scores were lower after 2017 (correlation −0.17), yet with controls the after switch is positive, +0.031, though only at the 10% level. Post-2017 reports come from smaller firms (−0.29 with size), with less upbeat prose (−0.25) and fewer tangible assets (−0.22): all traits that go with lower scores, size most strongly.
