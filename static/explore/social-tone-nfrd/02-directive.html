Why talk might count for less: three readings

The paper suggests three possible readings of the flatter line:

  • A ceiling. The rule lifted scores across the board, leaving less room for words to add.
  • A lost signal. Once every firm must cover the same topics, writing more no longer sets a firm apart. the toy
  • Cheap talk. Wording that firms choose freely and nobody verifies may simply be discounted.

What to take away

In 329 reports from 83 Italian firms, more social words went with better social scores; once the reports were compulsory, the link was weaker.

  1. Social tone is a count: the share of a report’s words on a list of social words, about two in every hundred, blind to whether the news is good or bad.
  2. Before 2017, comparing similar firms in the same industry, a point more of social tone went with about 0.10 more social score.
  3. After the directive the slope was about 0.06 smaller, significant at the 5% level; the paper suggests a ceiling or a lost signal.

An association, not a cause. Firms that do well may simply write more (footnote 10); the result does not hold when each firm is compared only with itself (footnote 8); and it rests on one country, 83 firms and about 60 reports from before the rule.

Open: the NFRD set no specific disclosure requirements. Would its successor, the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, make social talk informative again, and would the quality of the words tell raters more than their number?
