What happens when you rewrite the rules of an auction nobody can see?

An explorable explanation of The Market Quality of Decentralized Payment Systems by Sean Foley, Luca Galati, Ryan Riordan and Jiri Svec (working paper).

Every transaction on a blockchain has to win a place in the next block, and places are scarce. For years Ethereum sold them in a sealed auction: you named a fee without seeing anyone else's, and you either overpaid or waited. In August 2021 the network replaced that auction with a posted price that rises and falls block by block.

The paper measures what that change did, against Bitcoin as a control: what users paid, who received the money, and how much the network settled. The answers are not the ones the reform promised, and a second shock a year later shows it was the rules, not the upheaval.
