A second shock

Two doubts survive: perhaps any big protocol event shakes fees for a while, rules or no rules; or perhaps bidding moved into side deals that on-chain fees do not show.

The paper answers the first doubt with a second shock. For the second it mainly drops transactions that look like private fee deals and gets the same results.

On 15 September 2022 the Merge moved Ethereum from proof-of-work to proof-of-stake: block producers now lock up ether instead of racing computers, and are called validators (the paper uses the two words for one role). It removed the 2-ETH block subsidy and left the base fee, the tip and the elastic block as they were.

Easley, O'Hara and Basu (2019) predict that removing the subsidy leaves user fees alone, as long as blocks keep coming. Relative to Bitcoin, 30 days either side of the Merge, user fees…

Side trip · from A second shock

What about private fee deals?

The worry (p. 8): new fee rules may push users toward private channels such as Flashbots, paying the block producer off the books. Then the on-chain fees the paper measures would have changed in composition, not in cost.

The direct test (p. 40): drop every transaction that paid nothing, or less than the base fee in force, while still using gas, the footprint of a fee paid privately. Re-estimated on what is left, the results are economically and statistically similar.

Why not lean on the Merge? Because soon after it, block building moved to specialised builders through MEV-Boost, and part of the block producers' pay moved into builder payments: the private channel was changing during the placebo window itself (pp. 34–35).

Two shocks, one chart

Twelve outcomes, twice: the effect of EIP-1559 and of the Merge, each against Bitcoin. Tap a row for its numbers.

When the rules changed, user costs moved sharply. When the subsidy went, user costs did not move measurably; what collapsed was the subsidy itself, by about 96 per cent in the paper's records. Those records cover the execution layer only: staking rewards, about 1,600–1,700 ETH a day, moved to the consensus layer and are not in the data, so this is not the change in validators' total income (p. 34).

The Merge is a placebo for user costs only. Activity and welfare did move at the Merge, in the opposite direction to EIP-1559 (pp. 35, 38).

What it means

Replacing the sealed auction with a posted fee bought predictability inside the block and paid for it with higher, more dispersed fees, more pay for the block producer per transaction, and a smaller network, relative to Bitcoin. In the paper's words: lower fee uncertainty, traded for greater rent extraction and reduced welfare.

This is not a verdict for Bitcoin. Its auction still makes users overbid, and some of the activity that left Ethereum may simply have moved chains. The point is narrower and more useful: the rules that price and ration block space are a first-order determinant of what a payment network delivers, so a fee mechanism has to be judged on dispersion, congestion and the block producers' response, not on average fees or volatility alone.

What it cannot say

The paper lists its own limits.

  • Welfare is a settlement proxy · value transferred bounds surplus from above; the paper reads the contrast with Bitcoin, not the levels.
  • Dollar magnitudes are of their period · a proof-of-work era with gas prices far above today's; the mechanism conclusions carry over, the dollars do not.
  • One upgrade, five changes · EIP-1559 shipped in the London upgrade with four technical EIPs; strictly, the estimates are for London as a whole (fn. 5).
  • The Merge was a bundle · consensus, issuance and block building changed together, and MEV-Boost spread soon after; the placebo answers one question, whether a pay shock repriced block space for users.
  • Summer 2021 was not quiet · the NFT boom and DeFi on Ethereum, a mining-ban recovery on Bitcoin; controls, pseudo-event placebos and a six-chain panel address it, but nothing rules out a shock timed to the day.
  • Two chains · with Litecoin, Dogecoin, Dash and Zcash added as controls, the fee, dispersion, settled-value, contract-activity and welfare results survive; the volatility decline, the drops in confirmed transactions and in the queue, and the revenue results do not.
  • Robust where it can be · dropping transactions consistent with off-chain fee deals, errors robust to day-to-day correlation, 40-, 80- and 810-day windows, hourly data: the principal estimates hold.
