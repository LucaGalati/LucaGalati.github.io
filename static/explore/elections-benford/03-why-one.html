Revenue lingers among the 1s

In 1881 the astronomer Simon Newcomb noticed that the early pages of his book of logarithms wore out faster than the later ones: people looked up numbers starting with 1 far more often than those starting with 9. In 1938 the physicist Frank Benford checked the hunch on about 20,000 numbers from twenty unrelated sources.

Newcomb’s own explanation was that what is spread evenly is not the numbers but their logarithms. A growing company shows what that means. Going from €1 million to €2 million takes a doubling; going from €9 million to €10 million takes a rise of about 11%. At a steady growth rate, revenue lingers among the 1s and hurries through the 9s.

Change the toy company’s growth rate: the years change, the shares do not.
