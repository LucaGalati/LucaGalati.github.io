Who pays

A liquidity provider’s ledger

An option’s delta is how far its price moves when the index moves one point: at 0.35, about 35 cents of option price per point. Whoever takes the other side of an order now holds that bet on the market. It offsets the bet in E-mini S&P 500 futures and pays the futures spread to do so; a delta of 0.5 takes one E-mini.

So a liquidity provider, the market maker or other firm that fills the order, earns the effective spread and pays to hedge. What is left is its revenue. The twins of the last deck carry the same delta, 0.325, so they cost about the same to hedge: 4.24 cents and 4.15.

The provider pays for the improvement

Twins share a delta, so they cost the same to hedge; the difference, 0.09 to 0.29 cents, is not significant. The provider’s revenue is 4.42 cents lower on the auction trade without controls and 7.78 lower with them: at least as large as the fall in the effective spread.

One worry: liquidity providers might start auctions only for the orders cheapest to fill. If so, they would earn more there. They earn less.
