Twins, two weeks apart

Take every auction trade from the two weeks after February 22, 2021. For each one, find the nearest book trade from the two weeks before, on six characteristics: absolute delta, absolute vega, implied volatility, trade size, days to expiration and absolute gamma. One to one; no book trade is used twice.

Auction trades with no twin close enough are set aside: they are longer-dated, more directional and face quotes of about 50 cents. What remains are matched samples of 36,830 single-leg and 55,558 complex observations. The estimate speaks for the typical order: about two contracts, six days from expiry.

The placebo

The auction, or the week?

Auction trades and their twins come from different weeks. Perhaps execution simply got cheaper in late February. SPY options track one tenth of the same index and saw no change on February 22.

Over the same four weeks the single-leg SPY spread rose 19 percent without controls and fell 4 percent with them (+0.146 and −0.032 cents on a mean of 0.77). The first goes the wrong way for a market-wide fall; the second is a small fraction of the 54 percent fall in SPX.

A second bet

Do complex orders gain more?

A complex order is a package of options traded at one net price. It is about twice as likely to reach the SPX auction as a single-leg order of the same size and risk. If the auction draws complex orders so readily, surely it does the most for them?

What does the auction do to their cost?

You bet on more. The orders most drawn to it gain nothing.

You bet on about the same. In fact, nothing measurable.

You bet on less. Less, down to nothing measurable.

You bet on nothing. Right: nothing measurable.

A matched complex auction trade pays 2.95 cents against 2.60 for its twin: no improvement. The book already fills these orders at 0.18 of the quote, deep inside it, and so does the auction. There is little left to improve.
