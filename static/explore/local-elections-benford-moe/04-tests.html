Bigger piles flag smaller gaps

Fix one small touch-up, 3 statements in every 100 moved from first digit 1 to first digit 2, and grow the pile. The gaps stay the same, but both tests grow surer: the chi-square rises in step with the number of statements, the adjusted MAD with its square root. At the pre-election pile’s size the touch-up passes; at the ordinary pile’s size the chi-square flags it.

Critics call this the chi-square’s “excessive power” in large samples. The older remedy, the plain MAD, the average gap, does not grow with the pile. But even an honest pile’s MAD shrinks as the pile grows, so no single threshold suits piles of different sizes, as Cerqueti and Lupi show.

So the paper keeps the two tests and handles size another way: the bigger ordinary pile passes, and subsamples of the pre-election size show what chance alone produces.
