Locked in: which coins rose, which fell?

For about four days, the letter says, FTX let its customers trade but blocked almost all withdrawals. Money could hardly leave, but it could still change from one coin into another, at FTX’s own prices.

Four of the coins on offer: FTT, FTX’s own, as a warm-up; BNB, the coin of its rival Binance, in some ways the same kind of coin; and two stablecoins, Tether and DAI, built to hold a value of one dollar.

On FTX, in the collapse week, was each coin’s biggest swing against the dollar up or down? Bet on each chart.

From less to more trusted coins

The authors’ answer: demand for Tether, possibly from traders who wanted to cut their losses or to hold something “stable” (p. 5). A stablecoin is meant to hold its value, yet on FTX Tether traded, at times, well above a dollar. Meanwhile BNB, Binance’s own coin, rose as FTX’s collapsed.

Their reading: a flight to safety, from “less to more authoritative” coins, by traders who were mostly retail in the authors’ view and who ran not to cash or Treasury bonds but to coins most of the market saw as safer.

Some prices had started moving twelve days earlier

Look further back. Bitcoin, Ether, Dogecoin and Litecoin all began to climb about twelve days before the first report, Dogecoin most, and then fell with everything else once the collapse began.

The authors note the early rise and leave for future research whether anyone traded on early knowledge. Their suggestion for where to look: public blockchain records, which anyone can watch. During the crisis, a large FTT transfer by Binance, about $500 million, was spotted and posted online within moments.

What to take away

As FTT collapsed, its price moved more in step across nine venues; inside FTX, where almost nothing could be withdrawn, prices suggest money moved toward coins seen as safer.

  1. All 36 ties between the venues tightened in the collapse week, and each of FTX’s eight passed the letter’s strict test. The authors read this as contagion across every exchange in their sample.
  2. Minute by minute, the tie rose in lurches: a spike within hours of the report, then repeated jumps, with swings four to ten times wider than in the week before.
  3. Inside the frozen exchange, FTT fell, BNB and Tether rose, and DAI fell sharply before swinging back: in the authors’ reading, a flight from “less to more authoritative” coins.

One episode of twelve days on nine venues; the second finding is read from the letter’s figures; and the authors say their results cannot be carried over to calmer markets.

Left by the authors for future research: did anyone trade early on what was coming, and did the coins traders paid above market prices for go on to beat the market as a whole?
