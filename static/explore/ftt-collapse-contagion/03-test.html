A t of −44: the gap is 44 times its uncertainty

Take a tie measured many times before a shock and many times after. Single values wobble: in the toy, the thin line spans one standard deviation either side of each average, three times wider after the shock.

The test, though, is about the averages, and an average of n values is uncertain only by the wobble divided by √n: the thick mark. t is the gap between the two averages divided by the uncertainty of that gap. If nothing had changed, its size would pass about 3.3 only once in a thousand tries.

So when Table 2 reports t = −44.11 for FTX and CryptoCompare, the gap between the two weekly averages is about 44 times the uncertainty of that gap. With so many minutes, the authors chose a strict bar.

A tie that updates every minute

The letter’s model, a scalar BEKK GARCH, updates its estimate every minute. It tracks how much each venue swings and how much the two swing together, their co-movement; the tie is the co-movement scaled by the two venues’ own swings. Each update blends three ingredients:

new co-movement = (1 − a − b) × long-run co-movement + a × latest pair of surprises + b × previous co-movement

a sets how hard the estimate reacts to the newest minute, b how long it remembers. Both are positive and add up to less than one, which keeps the process stable. A big surprise shared by both venues makes the tie jump; then it sinks back at a pace set by memory. That is the saw-tooth of the letter’s Figure 2 (The tremor, slide 5).
