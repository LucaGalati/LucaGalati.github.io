What a tighter tie shows, and what it does not

What a correlation can say

  • No directiona correlation is symmetric: it shows that FTT moved more in step across venues; which venue moved first lies outside what it measures. The authors read the tightening as contagion set off by the FTT collapse (pp. 3 and 5).
  • Averages of a moving tiethe model’s tie changes every minute (Figure 2); the test sets its average in the week before against its average in the collapse week (Table 2).

How far the test reaches

  • One episodetwelve days around a single collapse. The authors say their findings cannot be carried over to wider, calmer periods (p. 7).
  • Uneven venuesLiquid’s returns do not average near zero, which the authors think may reflect scarce liquidity (Appendix); AAXE is known only by its data code (footnote 3); Poloniex was already swinging from 31 October (p. 3).

What the coin prices inside FTX can and cannot show

  • Read from a figurethis part rests on the cumulative returns drawn in Figure 3 and on how the authors read them (p. 5).
  • Prices, not flowsthe figure shows where prices went on FTX, not who bought or sold, or how much. That money moved toward safer coins is what the authors infer from those prices (p. 5).
  • When the doors shutthe letter speaks of four days in which FTX allowed trading but almost no withdrawals (p. 1); the shaded bands in this explorable place them before the last day of trading (footnote 4) and are approximate.
  • A locked roomalmost nothing could be withdrawn, though the authors note that some traders or hackers may have got assets off FTX all the same (pp. 1 and 7).
  • Who tradedthat the traders were mostly retail is what the authors suggest the pattern points to (p. 5).
  • Before the newsthe major coins rose about twelve days early; whether anyone traded on early knowledge the authors leave for future research (p. 5).
