Abnormal means ahead of its own normal

A price that rises is not, by itself, a reaction to news: some tokens drift up in most weeks. So an event study first asks what is normal for each token, and counts only what goes beyond it.

In the paper, a token’s normal is its average minute return from 1 June to 5 July. Its abnormal return each minute is its return minus that normal; the CAR adds them up from the start of the sample, 6 July.

Slide the normal and watch the CAR: the same prices give a rising line after a weak June and a falling one after a strong June. The benchmark is part of the result.

What the paper’s number compares

Table 2 sums up each token’s CAR line by its average height in each week. The paper’s measure is the difference: the week after minus the week before. Pick a shape; each is flat until the shot and ends the fortnight 20 points up, only the timing of the rise differs.

A jump right at the shot scores the full 20 points. A climb spread over the week after scores half as much, because the line spends most of that week below its final height; a jump followed by a climb lands in between. The measure records how high the line sits across the whole week, so height that arrives early counts for more.
