A memory for moving together

The paper’s main hypothesis is the authors’ second question: after the shot, did the tokens move more tightly together?

To follow that minute by minute it uses a BEKK model. Each minute the model updates the conditional covariance of two tokens from a long-run level, last minute’s two surprises multiplied together (positive when both jumped the same way; weight a) and last minute’s value (weight b).

Ht = (1 − a − b) · H̄ + a · et−1 e′t−1 + b · Ht−1

The test compares the line’s average before the shot with its average after. In the toy, the split in the middle plays the shot. Can one big jolt move an average? What has to change?

Equation (2), the scalar BEKK with variance targeting: H̄ is the whole sample’s covariance; a and b are positive, sum to less than 1 and stay the same over the whole sample (Section 3.2). The test: eq. (3), Section 4.1. Toy: surprises, jolt, split, a and b are illustrative values, not the paper’s estimates.

In Table 1: closer to Bitcoin for five, further for two

For each of the other nine tokens, the paper averages its BEKK covariance with Bitcoin over the week before the shot and over the week after, and tests the difference. Its t measures how far apart the two averages are relative to their noise; beyond about 3.3 clears the strict bar the paper sets, 0.1%.

Five moved more closely with Bitcoin: XRP most, then Shiba Inu, Dogecoin, Solana and Ethereum. Two moved less closely, Avalanche and Chainlink; Polkadot and Litecoin barely changed. The paper reads the rises as contagion, a jolt in one token reaching the others more easily, and notes that it was uneven, perhaps reflecting differences in fundamentals, liquidity and investor base.

Table 1: mean BEKK covariance with Bitcoin, 6–13 and 13–20 July, and the t-statistic; within each group, pairs are ordered by the change, computed from Table 1. The bar: Section 3.2 (α = 0.001, two-sided: |t| above about 3.29, the standard normal value). Litecoin’s t = −2.07 clears 5% but not that bar. The reading: pp. 3–4.

What to take away

In the week after the shot, the CAR lines of all ten tokens sat higher than in the week before: about 15 percentage points higher on average.

  1. The gains were universal but uneven: about 4 to 5 points for Polkadot and Avalanche, about 31 for XRP.
  2. In the paper’s reading, one direction across very different tokens is consistent with information-driven herding, not token-by-token revaluation; narrative-driven tokens gained most.
  3. In Table 1, Bitcoin’s co-movement rose with five of the other nine tokens, fell with two and held with two, which the paper reads as contagion, unevenly spread.

One event, one exchange, one week either side, no comparison market. XRP had news of its own; footnote 5 reports that the results stay qualitatively the same without XRP and over shorter and longer windows.

Most studies of crypto contagion follow bad news; this shock was read as good news. Would bad news line crypto up the same way, and how long does such a herd last?
