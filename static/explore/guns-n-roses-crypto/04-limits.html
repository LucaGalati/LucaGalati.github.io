What one shot can and cannot show

The paper calls the shot a plausibly exogenous event: nobody in the crypto market chose its timing, which is what makes it useful. A single event also sets limits on what it can show.

  • One eventNo second shot to compare with and no comparison market: crypto has no index, so each token is measured against its own June. Anything else that lifted crypto that fortnight would show up the same way.
  • XRP’s own newsXRP had begun to climb before the shot; the paper links that to a court ruling on its legal status. Rerun without XRP, the results are “qualitatively unchanged”.
  • WindowOne week either side in the tables; the paper reports that shorter and longer windows give similar results, with and without XRP.
  • One venueMid-prices from Gemini only, the largest tokens available there; USD Coin left out as a stablecoin.
  • Prices onlyThe data are prices, minute by minute: they show how the tokens moved, not who traded. Herding is read from the pattern of abnormal returns, which the paper finds consistent with a cascade.
  • A strict barWith 10,080 minutes in each week, the paper asks for significance at 0.1% rather than the usual 5%, to keep false alarms rare in a large high-frequency sample.
