A new coin goes from zero to hero overnight. What happens to the others?

At 02:44 UTC on 18 January 2025, the newly elected US president announced an official memecoin of his own, $TRUMP, minted on the Solana blockchain. A memecoin gives its holder no claim on anything; its price rests on attention alone.

A day later the First Lady’s $MELANIA followed. Markets had expected a friendlier line on crypto from the new presidency; here it was, in coin form.

So what does a boom in one brand-new coin do to the coins that were already trading? This letter follows ten of them, minute by minute, through the week before and the week after.

Launch minute: Section 2.1 and footnote 5. The 24-hour figures are CoinDesk’s, cited on page 1 (footnote 2), not estimates of the paper. $MELANIA and the expected policy stance: page 1.

Deck 1 · The launch · 2 of 4

Ten coins, every minute, one week either side

The authors take ten of the largest coins traded on Gemini, a US exchange, and record each one’s mid-price, halfway between the best offer to buy and the best offer to sell, at the close of every minute from 11 to 25 January 2025.

Split at the launch minute, that is one week before and one week after: 10,080 minutes on each side, 20,160 per coin. USD Coin is left out on purpose, since it is built to stay at one dollar. $TRUMP itself is not among the ten: the letter watches the coins around it.

Three things a boom could do to its neighbours

Three sketches, split by the launch minute illustrative

Swing harder

Each coin’s minutes get jumpier, as traders react to every headline.

Measured by how far a price usually moves in a minute (Table A.1).

Swing together

Coins begin to move in step, so a jolt to one is felt by the others. This is the spillover of the title.

Measured by a model of how each coin moves with Bitcoin, minute by minute (Table 1).

Trade places

Money leaves some coins for others: some gain while their neighbours lose.

Measured by gains and losses beyond each coin’s usual drift (Figures 2 and 3).

A boom could do all three at once. The letter looks at each, and so will you, in this order.
