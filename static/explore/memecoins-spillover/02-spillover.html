What “moving together” looks like

Watch two coins minute by minute. Some news hits both at once: a headline about crypto in general, a large trader selling everything. Other news hits only one of them. The larger the share of news they have in common, the more their minutes rise and fall together.

The paper’s model estimates that togetherness for each pair of coins, every minute. When it rises after a shock, a jolt to one coin travels to the other: a volatility spillover. When it spreads through a whole market, the authors speak of contagion.

Why it matters: when coins move in step, holding several of them spreads risk less than it seems. Financial stability, risk management and diversification are the stakes the paper names (page 2).

Invented coins and news, for illustration. The toy measures togetherness as the correlation of the two coins’ minute moves; the paper’s model estimates a conditional covariance for each pair (Section 2.2).

The third story is in prices. From every minute’s return the authors subtract the coin’s normal drift, its average minute return over 1 to 10 January, and add up what is left: the cumulative abnormal return. In the week before the launch most coins gained, likely, the paper suggests, on speculative anticipation of the new presidency: a fear of missing out.

What to take away

One coin’s launch moved the whole market, but not in one direction.

  1. In the week after the launch every coin swung harder, and most moved more closely with Bitcoin: in the authors’ reading, a volatility spillover and contagion across the market.
  2. The response was uneven. Solana, the new coin’s own chain, and Chainlink gained and drew closer to Bitcoin; Dogecoin’s and Shiba Inu’s abnormal returns were about −20% after the launch, likely as speculative money moved to the new token; XRP and Litecoin drifted away from Bitcoin.
  3. In the authors’ reading, politics moved this market: a divided public saw the president’s coin as an opportunity or as a political risk, and sentiment and policy narratives, not technology alone, shaped the response.

One event, ten coins on one exchange, a week either side, and no untouched coin to compare against; the reasons given for each coin’s path are the authors’ reading of the pattern.
