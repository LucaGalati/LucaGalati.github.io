Inside the bordering countries, kilometres stop mattering

The curve on the main path assumes every kilometre counts. For firms in the countries bordering Ukraine, the authors test that: they keep the border label and let distance add more on top. If closeness itself mattered, a firm 100 km from Ukraine should pay clearly more than one 600 km away.

It does not. The border step survives, at 0.08–0.11 percentage points; the extra slope inside the bordering countries is indistinguishable from zero. In the paper’s words, a national border “appears to summarize most of the cross-sectional heterogeneity”.

So across the whole sample closer firms did pay more, but near the war the pattern looks more like a step at the border than a smooth slope.
