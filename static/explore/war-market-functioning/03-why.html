The price of now

A share has no single price. At any moment there is a highest bid, what someone will pay right now, and a lowest ask, what someone will sell for right now. To trade immediately you cross the gap: buy at the ask, sell at the bid.

The quoted spread is that gap divided by the midpoint: the posted price of immediacy. The effective spread looks at real trades: how far each landed from the midpoint, counted one way. A buy at the ask pays half the quoted spread; a round trip, a buy and then a sell, pays that half twice.

Two reasons a dealer charges more

Whoever posts the bid and the ask is offering to trade with anyone who shows up, including people who know something.

Adverse selection

The likelier it is that the next order comes from someone better informed, the more the dealer loses to them on average, and the wider the spread must be to break even on everyone else (Glosten and Milgrom, 1985).

Inventory risk

Holding a position in a stock that may gap on the next headline is dangerous, and the dealer charges for carrying it (Stoll, 1978).

A war next door raises both: escalation, broken supply chains, regulatory intervention and macroeconomic instability are hard to judge from afar and easier to know about up close. That is how the paper reads its results, as consistent with these two channels, though it does not measure them directly.
