A large trade leaves two kinds of footprint

Follow the price around a large buy. Some of the move is the price of hurrying: to fill a big order fast you pay up, and once you are gone the price drifts back. That part is temporary. Some of it stays, because the market has concluded that the buyer knew something. That part is permanent, and it is the paper’s yardstick for how much information a trade carries.

The paper looks twenty trades either side. If the price had already moved before the trade, that is leakage: word got out first.

Book trades leak before they happen. Blocks do not.

Both paths start at the price twenty trades before and end twenty trades after. Blue is the average large trade on the book; gold the average block, which vanishes for ten minutes between execution and print.

Before a book trade the price is already moving its way: 0.133% for buys before the change. Large trades are “shopped” on the book first. Before a block, nothing.

This window runs from twenty trades before execution to twenty after the print, so it takes in the ten unseen minutes. That is why blocks look larger here than on the previous slide, which measured them around the print alone.

Deck 2 · Footprints · 4 of 4

Before the change, book trades left the deeper mark. After it, the gap was gone.

Same window, now the permanent footprint alone. Before December 2019 a book trade moved the price for good more than a block did, on both sides, by a gap significant at 1%. After it the two are level, and the gap can no longer be told apart from zero.

The gap closed in two different ways. For buys, book trades left a shallower mark, 0.211% down to 0.114%, while blocks stayed about where they were. For sells, blocks left a deeper one, 0.008% up to 0.117%, a change the paper’s before-and-after test on blocks finds significant at 5%.
