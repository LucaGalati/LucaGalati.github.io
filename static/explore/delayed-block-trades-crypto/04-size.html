A big order eats its way up the book

An order book is a queue of offers. Sellers post how much they will sell and at what price; the cheapest offer is first in line. A small buy takes the first offer. A large one takes the first, then the next, then the next, each a little dearer.

So the bigger the order, the further its price from the midpoint. Part of that is the cost of hurrying, and it fades as new sellers arrive. If others decide the buyer knew something, part of it stays. Price impact has been split this way since Kraus and Stoll (1972) and Scholes (1972), and the paper does the same.

A block facility offers another route: find someone to take the whole size at one price, away from the queue.
