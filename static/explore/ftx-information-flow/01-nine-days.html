An explorable explanation · De Blasis, Galati, Grassi & Rizzini

When FTX fell, which coins sat at the centre of the market?

On 2 November 2022 a news report suggested that Alameda Research, the trading firm run alongside the crypto exchange FTX, held mostly FTT, a token FTX had issued itself. Nine days later FTX filed for bankruptcy.

Prices record who lost. This paper looks at something prices alone do not show: the web of coins that rise and fall together, minute by minute, on FTX and on Binance. Think of it as a map of roads between coins. Some coins sit at the crossroads, where the market’s moves pass through. It matters which: a jolt at a crossroads reaches every other coin by the shortest roads, and a coin the market leans on is a weak point if it fails.

Which coins held that position before the collapse? Was it Bitcoin? And when FTT fell apart, did the centre move?

Nine days, timestamped

The authors cut their month of data at event (c), the moment Binance moved the FTT. The three weeks before it are the pre-collapse period; the days after it are the collapse. They chose the cut by looking at the price chart, where the break is plain.

FTT’s own price moves show it. During the collapse its one-minute returns swung about 22 times as widely as before, and its worst single minute took about a quarter off its price.

A link is two coins moving together

Line up two coins’ price moves, minute by minute. When they rise and fall in the same minutes their correlation is close to 1; when they move independently it is close to 0. Slide to see it.

The paper does this for every pair among 195 coins on FTX (and 324 on Binance). Each pair becomes a link whose strength is the size of its correlation, whatever its sign.

195 coins make 18,915 pairs, far too many to read. The map keeps 579 of them.
