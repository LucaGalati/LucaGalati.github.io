FTT kept three links, all to central coins

Before the collapse FTT had eleven links on the map, tied for fifteenth by degree. During it, three were left, to BTC, SOL and DOGE: the fewest any coin can have, since every coin enters the map attached to a triangle.

Yet it stayed in the closeness top twenty. Closeness asks only how short your roads are, and FTT’s three led straight to the busiest crossroads, like e’s single road in the toy. Information centrality asks whether the network would miss you. It would not: FTT fell out of that top twenty, which the authors read as investors hurrying to cut their ties to the token.

Table 7 and footnote 14; readings p.12. Eleven links: degree 0.05670 × 194, computed. The floor of three links is a property of the triangulated filter (Massara, Di Matteo and Aste 2017; see the side path on drawing the map), not stated in the paper. The paper does not name FTT’s eleven earlier neighbours, so the drawing does not say whether BTC, SOL and DOGE were among them.

The average turned after withdrawals stopped

A before-and-after comparison hides the timing. So the authors also rebuild the map every hour, each time from the previous 24 hours of returns, and follow the measures through the crisis.

Step through the events and watch the network’s average information centrality. It hardly moved while the news broke. It turned up only after FTX stopped withdrawals, climbed until FTT’s collapse and stayed high. The authors see a structural break in the plot after (h), and read it as investors cautiously selling and repositioning once their coins were stuck.

Section 3.5 and Fig. 6 (p.13–14): the break, p.13; the reading, p.14, consistent with an earlier study of trading on FTX by Galati, Webb and Webb. The paper prints these series only as plots, so the curve here is a schematic of the shape of Fig. 6, drawn by hand, without values.

What to take away

When FTT fell, the strongest links in FTX’s market loosened and the map re-formed around different coins.

  1. The centre was not simply Bitcoin: DOT and AVAX led before the collapse, DOGE on every measure during it. BTC was third on every measure before (level with SOL on closeness), second on two of three during it.
  2. FTT kept three links, to BTC, SOL and DOGE: still close to the centre, no longer a route the network needed.
  3. The clearest change in timing came after withdrawals stopped: a break the authors see in the hourly plots and read as investors repositioning.

What the paper reports are rankings and plots of a map built from one-minute correlations, with the month split at a date chosen from the price series. “Information” here is a property of the map, not observed messages.

Still open, as the authors note: what would a map that keeps the sign of each correlation add? And which coin would take the centre in the next exchange failure?
