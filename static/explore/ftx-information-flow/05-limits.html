Three weeks before, six and a half days during

The before-and-after comparison splits the month at event (c), when Binance moved the FTT. The authors chose that point from the price series, where the break is plain.

The two parts differ in length and in mood: about three weeks that already include the first news, against six and a half days in which FTT’s one-minute swings were about 22 times wider.

The paper also follows the market without the split. Table 5 reports the correlations over the whole month as well, and the hourly maps of Section 3.5 are each built from the 24 hours before them.

What a map of correlations cannot say

  • InformationHere it is a property of the map: a coin carries information when cheap roads of co-movement run through it. The paper observes prices, not messages, orders or traders.
  • SignCorrelations are counted by size, so two coins moving against each other look like two moving together. The authors report very few negative correlations, so that this choice does not affect their results, and name signed networks, harder to handle mathematically, as a fuller option for future work.
  • DataOne-minute prices from Refinitiv; the paper’s data statement says they are confidential.
