Keep the strongest links, in triangles

The authors filter the 18,915 pairs among 195 coins with a triangulated maximally filtered graph: keep the strongest links, as long as the map can be drawn flat with no two links crossing.

The rule is short. Start with the four coins most tied to all the others, joined in triangles. Then, again and again, find the coin and triangle with the strongest ties between them and put the coin inside, splitting the triangle into three. Slide to watch it on ten invented coins.

Every new coin brings exactly three links, so no coin ever has fewer than three, the floor FTT would hit. On FTX that leaves 579 of 18,915 pairs, about 3%; on Binance, 966 of 52,326.

Invented coins and correlations, for illustration. The filter: Sections 2.1 and 3.2 of the paper, after Massara, Di Matteo and Aste (2017); a filtered map of n coins has 3(n − 2) links. Counts computed: 195 × 194 ÷ 2 = 18,915 and 3 × 193 = 579; 324 × 323 ÷ 2 = 52,326 and 3 × 322 = 966.
